Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Across Santa Ana, Santa Ana Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
