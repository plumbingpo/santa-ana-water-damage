Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Santa Ana: What to Know

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

Our Santa Ana team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Our Process

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Extract the Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Check Around the Heater

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Dry and Monitor

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Stand and Subfloor Damage

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Adjacent Room Flooring

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Closet Walls

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Any rebuilding of the subfloor or platform beneath the tank ahead of the new heater.
  • Flooring type in the neighboring rooms, where the water reached them.
  • How long the tank kept refilling before the supply valve was closed.
  • The location of the heater, since a garage leak costs less than one in an interior closet or attic.
