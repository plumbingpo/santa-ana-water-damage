Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Santa Ana

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

Our Santa Ana crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Leave the Door ShutDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • Worn dishwasher door gaskets, cracked drain hoses or a failed pump seal.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Flooring and Subfloor

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Mold Behind Appliances

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. The wall behind the appliance is often the wettest because nobody could see it.
